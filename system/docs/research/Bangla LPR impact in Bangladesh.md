# Camera plate reading replaces Bangladesh's failing RFID

Bangladesh has already decided that cameras, not RFID tags, will identify its vehicles, and that decision gives a cheap, Bangla-accurate edge plate reader an immediate job. RFID stickers fail in about 90 % of reads at highway toll booths. Dhaka Metropolitan Police (DMP) switched on an ANPR case-filing system in May 2026 that reached 19 intersections and about 1,500 cases by 30 June, and the force wants 500 camera points but calls today's cameras too costly. BRTA has ordered front plates on 5.1 million motorcycles by 15 November 2026 and trialled them with AI cameras. The same reader can close leaks in tolling (Padma Bridge's ETC handles roughly 1 % of crossings), fake and stolen vehicles (about 78,000 reported on fake plates in 2025), per-vehicle fuel rationing (enforced in 2026 with paper receipts) and port gates (8,000 trucks a day at Chattogram). It cannot help with the millions of plateless battery rickshaws, and every use depends on access to BRTA's register. The right deployment runs inference at the edge and sends only text events plus a small crop over MQTT on dual-SIM 4G. That is about 6 GB and Tk 70-400 of data per camera per month, against about 1,300 GB to stream one 1080p feed. Starlink is reserved for 4G dead zones or sites where one dish serves many cameras, and a Raspberry Pi 5 with a Hailo NPU (about 7-8 W) is the best power and cost fit for solar poles. The OCR should stay off uncalibrated INT8, because the team's dynamically quantised reader lost 20 points. Plate reads tied to time and place count as personal data under the Personal Data Protection Act 2026. Private operators therefore need notice, a lawful basis and short retention, while police use falls under broad Section 24 exemptions. The publishable novelty is not another YOLO-plus-OCR pipeline but what no Bangla plate paper offers yet: a multi-frame track benchmark, calibrated "send to a human" abstention, grammar-constrained decoding and a gated vision-language fallback. These are also the features that make an automated fine notice trustworthy.

## Bangladesh has already bet on cameras over RFID tags

Bangladesh's vehicle problem is first a motorcycle problem. BRTA counted about **6.85 million registered motor vehicles by August 2026, about 5.1 million of them motorcycles**. Over 2.4 million are registered in Dhaka, and about 800 new motorcycles are added each day ([Prothom Alo, 25 Oct 2026](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/f5iiqyoxwt)). Outside that register sit an estimated 4.5 to more than 6 million battery-run rickshaws and autorickshaws ([Dhaka Tribune, 2026](https://www.dhakatribune.com/business/408819/battery-run-rickshaws-strain-bangladesh-s-power)). The resulting costs are large but poorly measured. A 2023 NBER study of more than 1,200 cities ranked Dhaka the slowest ([Dhaka Tribune, 2023](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/326673/dhaka-traffic)), and BUET put the average speed on its main roads at 4.8 km/h ([Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/city/ejgqmk4a0p)). Congestion-cost estimates run from **Tk 370 billion a year (BUET's Accident Research Institute, 2018)** ([Financial Express, 2018](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/national/traffic-congestion-in-dhaka-causes-tk-370b-annual-loss-1526745105)) to about 2.9 % of GDP in a 2021 BIDS paper ([Financial Express](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/home/traffic-gridlock-causes-huge-financial-losses-1608994056)). No estimate newer than 2021 was found. Road-death counts are just as uncertain. BRTA counted 5,490 deaths in 2025 ([Bonik Barta, 21 Jan 2026](https://en.bonikbarta.com/bangladesh/gyWM582NKumWKeL8/)) and the Road Safety Foundation 7,359 ([Prothom Alo, 10 Jan 2026](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/accident/35tuiqg3oi)). WHO's model estimated **31,578 deaths for 2021, about six times the police count** ([Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/381561/who-road-crashes-claiming-young-lives-in)). A six-fold gap between official and modelled deaths shows that the state lacks reliable vehicle-level records.

The tool meant to provide those records has not worked. BRTA's 2012 digital number-plate project paired retro-reflective, embossed plates with an RFID tag ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Bangladesh)). Yet **RFID stickers fail in about 90 % of cases at electronic toll booths, and at Jamuna Bridge only 15 % of vehicles are detected automatically** ([Prothom Alo, 25 Mar 2025](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/3nuoe4u5v8)). In May 2026 DMP warned that many vehicles run without BRTA plates or with inactive tags ([The Daily Star, 11 May 2026](https://thedailystar.net/news/governance/news/action-against-vehicles-without-brta-issued-number-plates-rfid-tags-dmp-4173456)). Police also list painted numbers, plates paid for but never collected, and failed tags among their problems ([Dhaka Tribune, 17 May 2026](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/dhaka/410422/ai-cameras-penalty-points-system-reshape-dhaka)). A May 2025 report, from a single source, put **78,000 vehicles on fake plates**, with documents for nearly 600,000 vehicles yet to be updated ([Daily Ittefaq, 26 May 2025](https://en.ittefaq.com.bd/12055/how-do-vehicles-run-with-fake-number-plates)). By October 2026 Prothom Alo judged that RFID had not delivered its promised anti-counterfeiting, stolen-vehicle and tolling benefits, and noted that nobody had established why ([Prothom Alo, 25 Oct 2026](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/f5iiqyoxwt)).

In 2026 the state moved the identification job to cameras. DMP's AI-based violation-detection system went live on **7 May 2026**. It reads plates, matches them to the BRTA register and notifies owners by SMS and registered post ([Dhaka Tribune, 7 May 2026](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/dhaka/409621/ai-based-traffic-case-system-goes-live-in-dhaka)). Ittefaq instead dates operation from 7 April, with enforcement stepped up in May ([Ittefaq, 19 May 2026](https://en.ittefaq.com.bd/16609/can-ai-cameras-fix-dhaka%E2%80%99s-traffic-mess)). The pilot cost **about USD 50,000, half for software and half for servers, storage and cameras**. Its software is credited to a BUET team, and case filing was still partly manual ([Arab News, 28 Jun 2026](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2648890/world)). Global Voices, by contrast, describes cases being generated without human involvement, so how automated the system is remains disputed ([Global Voices, 3 Jun 2026](https://globalvoices.org/2026/06/03/ai-cameras-are-surveilling-the-roads-in-dhaka-bangladesh/)). By 30 June it covered **19 intersections and had produced about 1,500 cases and nearly 38,000 summonses** ([TBS, 30 Jun 2026](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/ai-run-traffic-cameras-now-19-dhaka-intersections-1500-cases-filed-so-far-dmp-chief)). The stated target is at least 500 camera points ([Global Voices](https://globalvoices.org/2026/06/03/ai-cameras-are-surveilling-the-roads-in-dhaka-bangladesh/)). Highway Police followed. **1,427 AI cameras are planned at 490 points on the Dhaka-Chattogram highway**, and an auto-fine system there issued 449 cases in its first 24 hours after launching on 18 August 2026 ([TBS/BSS, 11 Sep 2026](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/1539861)). BRTA then made **motorcycle front plates mandatory by 15 November 2026**. They are non-RFID plates, white with black Bangla characters at least 20 mm high ([bdnews24, 16 Oct 2026](https://bdnews24.com/bangladesh/trrte0u6fs)), and sample plates were tested with AI cameras on 19 August and 5 September ([Prothom Alo, 25 Oct 2026](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/f5iiqyoxwt)). A 12-point penalty scheme is planned that would move enforcement from the vehicle to the driving licence, although BRTA's software for it is still in development ([Dhaka Tribune, 17 May 2026](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/dhaka/410422/ai-cameras-penalty-points-system-reshape-dhaka)).

Officials have said exactly where the current system breaks. Some plates are too small or unclear to read, and plateless battery rickshaws appear in no database ([TBS, 24 Jun 2026](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/1470786)). Some notices went to previous owners because ownership transfers were never recorded ([TBS, 30 Jun 2026](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/ai-run-traffic-cameras-now-19-dhaka-intersections-1500-cases-filed-so-far-dmp-chief)). Scam callers impersonate DMP to collect fake camera fines ([TBS, 24 Jun 2026](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/1470786)). In July a senior DMP traffic officer called the cameras too costly for a wide rollout and asked for cheaper plug-and-play units ([1001 Smart Cities](https://1001smartcities.org/projects/dhaka-e-traffic-prosecution/)). **No read-accuracy figure for the DMP system has been published**, so no outside system, the team's included, can be compared with it on public evidence. History adds a warning. World Bank-funded signals at 68 sites had failed by 2009, another 91 intersections never became operational, and JICA-funded AI signals stopped working soon after installation ([Bonik Barta, 18 Jun 2026](https://en.bonikbarta.com/bangladesh/pr9vkrHF2gQOeBrq)). In Bangladesh, cost per camera and the burden of upkeep decide success as much as recognition accuracy does.

## Seven problems a Bangla plate reader can address, and two it cannot

The table maps each use the brief names to the pain that has been documented, what an edge reader adds, and the institutional dependency that limits it. The pains are reported facts. The "adds" column is a set of proposals, because apart from DMP and the highway auto-fine system, no Bangladeshi plate-based deployment was found for any of these uses.

| Use | Documented pain | What an edge Bangla reader adds | What it depends on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Traffic enforcement (DMP, Highway Police, BRTA) | 19 intersections against a 500-point target; unreadable, small and painted plates; DMP calls cameras too costly | Cheap units per approach; two-line rear and new single-line front plates; abstaining on doubtful reads | BRTA register access; ownership-transfer backlog |
| Tolling (RHD, Bridge Authority, expressway operators) | ETC about 6 % of RHD bridge traffic (Feb 2025) and about 1 % at Padma; manual booths 300-400 vehicles an hour | Camera backup when the tag fails, plate-to-account matching, and an independent audit count | ETC account and bank integration; contractor cooperation |
| Fake, cloned and stolen vehicles | About 78,000 vehicles on fake plates (2025); 1,123 stolen vehicles recovered by DMP from 2019 to 2021 | Hotlist matches, class-letter vs vehicle-type mismatches, the same plate seen in two places at once | Police hotlists and BRTA data; human confirmation of hits |
| Fuel rationing | 2026 caps enforced with paper receipts; ride-share bikes checked against the app by hand | A plate log at pump entry, shared between stations | A national data-sharing mandate, which raises privacy concerns |
| Ports and land ports | About 8,000 vehicles a day at Chattogram; digital gate pass already in use | Binding each pass to the truck that actually enters; catching reused passes | Integration with the port authority's pass system |
| Parking (city, malls, hospitals, campuses) | App-only pilots, no ANPR; a huge shortfall of spaces | Ticketless entry and exit; evidence of illegal parking | Notice and lawful basis under the data law |
| Ride-sharing and fleets | Ownership and registration-age rules broken; off-app rides leave no record | A plate-verification API at driver onboarding and in yards | No Bangladeshi fleet data found |
| Plateless vehicles | 4.5 to 6+ million battery rickshaws, largely unregistered | Nothing; there is no plate to read | Registration policy |
| Wrong-owner records | Notices sent to previous owners | Nothing; the error is in the register | BRTA data quality |

**Tolling is where the money is most visible.** Padma Bridge carried 26.86 million vehicles and collected Tk 3,429.45 crore up to 29 June 2026 ([Ittefaq, 1 Jul 2026](https://en.ittefaq.com.bd/17181/padma-bridge-toll-revenue-reaches-tk-3-429cr)), about Tk 1,277 per vehicle on average. Its RFID-based ETC had 1,008 registered vehicles and 56,406 crossings by 27 July 2026 ([UNB, 27 Jul 2026](https://unb.com.bd/category/Bangladesh/padma-bridge-etc-toll-revenue-surpasses-tk-10-crore-vehicle-registrations-top-1000/191657)). Against roughly 20,000 crossings a day, that is **about 1 % of traffic over ten months** by my calculation. Across RHD's network, only 17 of more than 71 toll bridges have ETC, each on a single lane, and the Financial Express names separate software systems as one reason registrations stay low ([Financial Express, 26 May 2026](https://today.thefinancialexpress.com.bd/print/etc-reg-surges-ahead-of-eid-1779729204)). In February 2025 only 6 % of vehicles on RHD's major bridges paid electronically. A manual booth clears at most 300-400 vehicles an hour, and BUET's Md Hadiuzzaman estimates automation could raise that fourfold ([Prothom Alo, 25 Mar 2025](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/3nuoe4u5v8)). A camera reader can act as the backup identifier when the tag fails, and it gives an independent vehicle count where contractors take large shares. About 80 % of Dhaka-Bhanga expressway toll revenue has gone to its Korean operator, and an inquiry reportedly confirmed inflated salary claims for Padma toll collectors ([Prothom Alo, 13 Nov 2024](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/992xo4qdpg)). The Karnaphuli tunnel carries only 4,000-4,500 vehicles a day against a forecast of 28,350 and runs a deficit of about Tk 36.5 crore a year ([TBS, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/infrastructure/tk10689cr-karnaphuli-tunnel-struggling-close-revenue-gap-amid-thin-traffic)). That makes it a low-risk pilot site where every vehicle could be read and audited.

**Fake and stolen vehicles can be checked as soon as a plate read can be looked up, and some fakes can be caught without any database.** The class letter on a Bangladeshi plate encodes vehicle type and engine size. For example, অ marks motorcycles up to 100 cc, ক cars up to 1,000 cc and ঢ tankers and articulated vehicles ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Bangladesh)). A reader paired with a light vehicle-type classifier can therefore flag a truck-class letter on a private car. With BRTA access it can also flag numbers that are unregistered, registered to a different vehicle, seen at two distant places at once, or on a hotlist. Theft is real but poorly counted: DMP recovered 1,123 stolen vehicles, 545 of them motorcycles, between January 2019 and July 2021 ([Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/crime-and-law/stolen-bike-network-all-over-the-country)), and no national figures for 2023-2026 were found. The United States shows the risk. Flock Safety's solar-powered LTE readers are deployed in thousands of communities, and wrongful hotlist hits have led to detentions ([Wikipedia: Flock Safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flock_Safety)). A hit must therefore trigger human confirmation, not an automatic stop.

**The 2026 fuel crisis showed that the pump has no per-vehicle identity.** BPC's directive of 6 March capped motorcycles at 2 litres and cars at 10 litres a day, and enforced it by asking buyers to show their previous receipt, while queues stretched for blocks ([Prothom Alo, 6 Mar 2026](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/u2rd23k06t)). Four days later, ride-share motorcycles were allowed 5 litres, but only after their registration number was matched by hand against the ride-hailing app ([Prothom Alo, 10 Mar 2026](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/wzm1k9w3pe)). In Chattogram, tag officers were deployed at stations and customers waited more than 30 minutes ([TBS, 28 Mar 2026](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/fuel-rationing-underway-ctg-filling-stations-after-deployment-tag-officers-1396086)). A paper receipt cannot stop a buyer from going to a second station. A plate log at pump entry, shared between stations, can, and it would automate the ride-share check as well. It would also be a national vehicle-movement log, exactly the kind of state processing that the data law's exemptions allow and its critics worry about.

Ports and commercial sites follow the same logic on a smaller evidence base. At Chattogram port about 8,000 vehicles pass each day. An online gate pass introduced in February 2025 cut entry time from 15-20 minutes to 1-2 minutes, and nearly 100,000 drivers and helpers are registered with photos ([TBS, 18 Feb 2025](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/1071451)). Reading the plate at the gate ties each pass to the truck that actually enters. Benapole land port has seen queues of 5,000-10,000 trucks in older reports ([Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/business/local/5000-trucks-waiting-to-enter-bangladesh-through-benapole)), but no data from 2023-2026 were found. Dhaka's 330,968 private cars in 2019 would need about 80 million square feet of parking ([The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/city/news/8-crore-sqft-needed-just-cars-study-1878253)). Yet smart parking so far is app-based: DNCC's 2023 pilot covers 202 street spots in Gulshan with no ANPR ([The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/tech-startup/news/dncc-launches-smart-parking-app-3464746)), and only MicrotechBD was found advertising ANPR-integrated car parks ([MicrotechBD](https://www.microtechbd.net/intelligent-car-parking-solution.php)). BRTA rules allow a vehicle to be used for ride-sharing only if it is personally owned and has been registered for at least a year, and operators have broken them ([Financial Express](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/trade/ridesharing-companies-breach-registration-rules-1546763800)). A plate-verification check at driver onboarding could enforce those rules. For logistics fleets, garment-factory gates, campuses and gated housing, no Bangladeshi market or incident data were found, so the case for those uses rests on ALPR's record elsewhere, not on local evidence.

**Two problems sit beyond any camera's reach.** A reader cannot identify a vehicle that has no plate, and it cannot correct an owner record that was never transferred. Both are registration and database problems. The value of every use in the table rises or falls with BRTA's willingness to share and clean its register.

## Send text, not video: an edge-first design for Bangladeshi roads

### Events need about 200 times less bandwidth than streaming

One 1080p H.264 stream runs at a typical 2-8 Mbps ([VXG](https://help.videoexpertsgroup.com/kb/recommended-bitrate-settings); [AWS Rekognition](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/rekognition/latest/dg/recommendations-camera-stored-streaming-video.html)). An edge event is a few hundred bytes of JSON (plate, time, GPS, confidence) plus an optional 5-20 KB JPEG crop. The table below assumes 20,000 reads per camera per day. That load is my assumption, since no Bangladeshi per-lane counts were found. Data costs are priced at August 2026 prepaid rates: Banglalink 10 GB for 30 days at Tk 109 (about Tk 10.9/GB) up to Grameenphone 1 GB for 7 days at Tk 48 ([Daily Observer, 4 Aug 2026](https://observerbd.com/news/586298)). No IoT/M2M tariffs were published.

| Mode (one camera) | Data per month | Average bitrate | 4G data cost per month |
|---|---|---|---|
| Text events only (~0.5 KB) | 0.3 GB | ~1 kbps | one minimum pack (Tk 109) |
| Text + 10 KB plate crop | 6.3 GB | ~19 kbps | Tk 69-217 |
| Text + 20 KB plate crop | 12.3 GB | ~38 kbps | Tk 134-424 |
| 1080p H.264 at 4 Mbps | 1,296 GB | 4 Mbps | Tk 14,100-44,700 |

The streaming row fails on capacity before it fails on cost. Bangladesh's median mobile upload was 11.36 Mbps in Ookla's January 2024 data ([The Daily Star, 27 Feb 2024](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/economy/news/bangladesh-slumps-global-mobile-internet-rankings-3553121)), so one 4 Mbps feed would take a third of a typical user's upload on a shared cell. Scaled to the 1,427 planned Dhaka-Chattogram cameras, streaming would need about 5.7 Gbps sustained and 1.85 PB a month. Events with crops would need about 9 TB a month. **Edge recognition is therefore the only design that both the networks and the budgets can support.** Payload choices such as crop size and JPEG quality matter more than the choice of protocol.

### Dual-SIM 4G is the backbone; Starlink is the exception

On paper, 4G reaches about 95 % of the population ([The Daily Star, 20 Jan 2026](https://www.thedailystar.net/news/bangladeshs-5g-moment-promise-challenges-and-the-road-ahead-4085521)). Highways tell a different story. In BTRC's July 2025 drive test over 574 km of Tangail, Bogura and Gaibandha, **the share of tested areas without 4G was 41 % for Robi, 49 % for Banglalink, 58 % for Grameenphone and 77 % for Teletalk**. That is despite 2018 licence terms that required 4G on national highways by 2023, and the operators dispute the method ([Dhaka Tribune, 4 Oct 2025](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/393181/btrc-finds-gaps-in-4g-coverage-operators-fail-to)). Where 4G exists it is more than enough. Median mobile latency was 25 ms in January 2024 ([The Daily Star, 27 Feb 2024](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/economy/news/bangladesh-slumps-global-mobile-internet-rankings-3553121)), and median download reached about 42 Mbps by March 2026 ([Wikipedia, citing Speedtest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_Internet_connection_speeds)). An event stream averages around 20 kbps. 5G adds nothing essential. Only Grameenphone and Robi offer it, mainly in divisional cities, and Banglalink had not launched as of August 2026 ([The Daily Star, 26 Aug 2026](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/news/5g-ready-handsets-gain-ground-users-upgrade-4256911)). GSMA lists Grameenphone NB-IoT as Bangladesh's only mobile-IoT network, with no LTE-M ([GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/iot/mobile-iot-commercial-launches/)). BTRC plans a separate "06" number series for data-only IoT SIMs and has asked for them to be exempted from the Tk 300 SIM tax ([The Daily Star, 4 Dec 2025](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/news/btrc-introduce-separate-sim-series-iot-m2m-devices-4050931)). The defensible design is a **dual-SIM router on two operators** (for example Grameenphone plus Robi), on plain LTE.

Starlink covers the holes, at a price in money and in power. BTRC issued its two 10-year licences on 29 April 2025 ([TBS, 29 Apr 2025](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/1129576)), and commercial service began on 20 May 2025 ([TBS/UNB, 20 May 2025](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/starlink-ca-yunus-greets-all-involved-1147181)). Residential plans cost Tk 4,200-6,000 a month and business plans start at Tk 4,600 ([Ajker Patrika, 6 Jul 2026](https://www.ajkerpatrika.com/en/technology/ajpmhyhwwvpns)). The "Local Priority" tiers run from 50 GB at Tk 4,600 to 2 TB at Tk 38,800, according to search snippets of the official page that should be confirmed before quoting ([Starlink BD](https://starlink.com/en-bd/business/service-plans)). A Mini kit retails at Tk 24,900 ([Star Tech](https://www.startech.com.bd/starlink)), so one site's first year costs about **Tk 80,100** (the kit plus 12 months of Local Priority 50 GB) before mounts and power. Measured performance conflicts. Ookla reported a median of 88.95 Mbps down with 35 ms latency for Q4 2025 ([The Daily Star, 14 Apr 2026](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/economy/news/starlink-bangladesh-blazing-fast-few-can-afford-4151116)). A crowdsourced July 2026 sample showed 27 Mbps down and **5.4 Mbps up** at the median ([tnetweather](https://tnetweather.com/starlink-coverage/bangladesh/)). Either figure is ample for events and too little for more than one video stream. No rule bars government or business use. Corporate and government clients buy through resellers, including Robi, Felicity IDC and the state-owned BSCL ([The Daily Star, 9 Sep 2025](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/news/starlink-establishes-four-local-gateways-3982116)). BTRC has, however, flagged that Starlink's lawful-interception tool was "not working as expected", a friction point for police clients ([Prothom Alo, 2 Nov 2025](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/hb1rntaehr)). The decisive cost is power. A Starlink Mini averages 25-40 W ([CDW](https://www.cdw.com/product/starlink-mini-kit/8388748)), several times what a complete Pi-class recognition unit draws. Starlink therefore pays only at 4G dead zones (hill tracts, char and haor roads) or at shared sites such as a toll plaza, where one dish serves many cameras.

The other links are fallbacks at most. Bangladesh Satellite-1 (GEO) VSAT is sold by BSCL with no published tariff ([BSCL](https://bscl.gov.bd/?p=19)), and geostationary physics sets a round trip of at least about 480 ms. That is still fine for alerts measured in seconds. LoRaWAN has **no frequency plan or regulatory document for Bangladesh** on The Things Network's country table ([TTN](https://www.thethingsnetwork.org/wiki/LoRaWAN/Frequencies/By-Country)). Its payload ceiling is 51 bytes at low data rates and 222 at the highest ([TTN EU868](https://www.thethingsnetwork.org/docs/lorawan/regional-parameters/eu868/)), and TTN's fair-use limit is 30 seconds of uplink airtime per device per day ([TTN forum](https://www.thethingsnetwork.org/forum/t/the-things-network-fair-use-policy/47689)). The Bengali plate string alone takes roughly 50-60 bytes in UTF-8. A binary event (district index, class letter, six BCD digits, timestamp, coordinates, camera ID) packs into about 20 bytes and fits. A JPEG crop does not. LoRaWAN can serve, at most, as a text-only alert and heartbeat channel on a private gateway, and it carries regulatory risk. Toll plazas and control buildings probably have fibre, given a backbone of about 142,000 km ([The Daily Star, 20 Jan 2026](https://www.thedailystar.net/news/bangladeshs-5g-moment-promise-challenges-and-the-road-ahead-4085521)). No source, however, documents the backhaul at any toll plaza, and the Dhaka-Chattogram camera plan does not say how its cameras connect ([TBS/BSS, 11 Sep 2026](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/1539861)).

### MQTT with an on-device outbox rides out drop-outs and outages

For small events over cellular or satellite links, MQTT has the leanest framing: a 2-byte fixed header ([HiveMQ](https://www.hivemq.com/article/mqtt-vs-http-protocols-in-iot-iiot)), against the 800-1,000 bytes of headers a typical HTTP request and response carry ([Kaa IoT](https://www.kaaiot.com/iot-knowledge-base/mqtt-vs-http-for-iot-device-communication-performance-comparison-and-best-practices)). On a 400-byte event, that is the difference between about 10 % overhead and more than double the payload. Broker persistence is not enough on its own. EMQX's v5 persistent sessions are held in memory ([EMQX docs](https://docs.emqx.com/en/emqx/v5.0/mqtt/mqtt-session-and-message-expiry.html)), and one user lost the oldest messages once a long outage exceeded the default 1,000-message queue ([EMQX forum](https://www.emqx.io/forum/t/how-to-detect-queue-overruns-for-persistent-sessions/179)). The recommended pattern is therefore MQTT 5 at QoS 1 over a persistent TLS session, with the text event published first on a high-priority topic and the crop sent second, referenced by event ID. **A durable SQLite or flash outbox on the device** covers the gap before the broker sees a reconnect. Bangladesh needs that resilience. Power cuts run 2-3 hours a day in cities and 4-6 in rural areas ([The Daily Star, 20 Jan 2026](https://www.thedailystar.net/news/bangladeshs-5g-moment-promise-challenges-and-the-road-ahead-4085521)). The July-August 2024 internet shutdown was the country's longest ([OONI, 2025](https://ooni.org/post/2025-bangladesh-report/)), although the Telecommunication (Amendment) Ordinance approved in December 2025 prohibits future shutdowns ([Meatechwatch, 25 Dec 2025](https://meatechwatch.com/2025/12/25/bangladesh-approves-telecommunication-ordinance-2025-guarantees-no-internet-shutdowns/)). For a stolen-car alert, the network adds well under a second on 4G or Starlink, even with a TLS reconnect. A **five-second read-to-alert target**, the figure UK ANPR proponents cite for hotlist checks ([Queensland Parliament submission](https://documents.parliament.qld.gov.au/com/TC-C2D2/IANPR-93E1/submissions/00000026.pdf)), is realistic. The real risks are coverage holes and the unmeasured last hop to an officer's phone, not bandwidth. Every event should carry its capture time, so that a hit recorded in a dead zone arrives flagged as stale instead of being lost.

Every accelerator on the market runs a nano detector at 640 px far faster than the 25-30 frames per second a roadside camera delivers. The real choice turns on power, price and the OCR stage.

The team's own measurements decide the OCR question. In the edge-deployment study (Kaggle run of 26 October 2026), the YOLOv8n-576 plate detector reached mAP50 99.0, and 98.1 under TensorRT INT8. INT8 was not faster than FP16 on a T4 (5.6 vs 5.2 ms). **Dynamic INT8 quantisation of the slot OCR, however, cut exact-plate accuracy from 57.60 % to 37.33 %.** End to end, the pipeline ran at 47.2 FPS on a T4 and **13.4 FPS on a 4-thread CPU** (team notes). The YOLO26 study found the NMS-free YOLO26-N 1.62 times faster end to end than YOLO11-N on a 4-thread CPU (15.9 + 0.3 ms against 18.5 + 7.7 ms) (team notes). These timings belong to a weaker reader than the one that reaches 90 %. The accurate configuration (the four-reader vote or the char-YOLO + SVTRv2 fallback) has **not yet been timed on edge hardware**. SVTRv2-T alone took 13.6 ms per crop and the CRNN 18.6 ms on 4 threads of the team's local PC (team notes), and a Pi 5's cores will be slower. The design that follows is to run the detector (and possibly char-YOLO) on the Hailo NPU, where calibrated INT8 costs well under a point. The sequence recogniser should run in FP32 or FP16 on the CPU, and only on plates the first reader is unsure of, until static INT8 with in-domain calibration or quantisation-aware training is shown to hold up. Jetson is the alternative if toolchain risk matters more than watts. TensorRT FP16 needs no calibration, but NVIDIA's July 2026 price rise (+33 % to +101 %) took the cheapest kit to USD 399, and TensorRT 10.3 on JetPack 6 blocks INT8 builds for NMS-free outputs such as YOLO26's ([Ultralytics Jetson guide](https://docs.ultralytics.com/guides/nvidia-jetson/)). Local supply is thin. Techshop BD listed no Jetson and no Hailo product, and its Pi 5 8 GB price of Tk 35,650 is about 3.6 times the USD 80 RRP at an assumed 122 Tk per dollar, so both routes need import quotes.

### Solar: about 200 W of panel for a Pi-class unit, double that for Jetson, more with Starlink

Bangladesh receives about 3 kWh/m²/day in December and 5 in April, with a monsoon dip from May to September ([JMERD, BUET](https://jmerd.me.buet.ac.bd/archive/volume_16/JMERD_paper_vol_16_page_41-46.pdf)). Monsoon cloud can cut irradiance dramatically from June to September ([profilesolar](https://profilesolar.com/locations/Bangladesh/Kishorganj/)). My sizing assumes 3.5 sun-hours, a 0.7 derate, three days of autonomy and LiFePO4 at 80 % depth of discharge. The camera-with-IR (about 8 W) and router (about 4 W) figures are assumptions, not sourced. On those terms, a Pi 5 + Hailo unit at about 20 W uses 480 Wh a day and needs **a panel of about 200 W and about 1.8 kWh of battery**. A Jetson unit in 15 W mode needs about 265 W and 2.4 kWh. Adding a Starlink Mini at 25-40 W adds roughly 245-390 W of panel, which more than doubles the site. Commercial practice supports the low end. Milesight's 4G solar ANPR camera draws 2.43-5.61 W on a 100 W panel with 5-9 days of battery backup ([Milesight](https://www.milesight.com/security/product/solar-powered-anpr-road-traffic-camera)). A fanless IP66 enclosure with a sun shield and a breather vent suits a 7-8 W Pi unit in Bangladeshi heat and humidity far better than a 25 W Jetson. No Bangladeshi enclosure temperature data were found.

### Shutter, angle and infrared matter more than model size

Plate capture fails at the camera before it fails in the model. Hikvision's LPR installation guide calls for a view within 30° of the travel path, plate tilt within ±5° and a shutter of 1/1000-1/2000 s ([Hikvision](https://cdn.connectec.uk/Files/Products/CCTV/Camera/Hikvision_Trade/installation_guide_of_hikvision_lpr_camera.pdf)). Trassir asks for 120-140 px across the plate and angles of 15° or less, 30° at most ([Trassir](https://storage.trassir.com/files/Camera%20setup%20checklist%20for%20AutoTRASSIR.pdf)). Sighthound's much slower 1/100 s starting point conflicts with both ([Sighthound](https://support.sighthound.com/kb/article/223-alpr-camera-guidelines)). That guidance was written for Latin plates. A 285 mm front plate imaged at 130 px resolves a 20 mm Bangla character in only about 9 px. The team should therefore aim above the guidance for two-line Bangla glyphs and measure exact-match accuracy against plate width in pixels on its own data. Night is the weakest point: the slot OCR read only **27.7 % of night plates** (team notes). In the HVI-CIDNet study, zero-shot low-light enhancement did not help (night crops read 40 → 36, not significant), and enhancing every crop with HVI-CIDNet cost 20 of 586 day plates (team notes). The fix belongs at capture, with 850 nm IR illumination and a manual fast shutter. Generic Hikvision IP cameras (2-6 MP ColorVu) cost Tk 4,273-6,450 in Bangladesh ([BDStall](https://www.bdstall.com/brand/hikvision/)). A dedicated Hikvision ANPR bullet is listed at Ryans without a price, and the same family sells for ₹78,000 in India ([IndiaMART](https://m.indiamart.com/proddetail/ip-camera-27632006330.html)). A cheap camera plus a separate IR illuminator is the sensible pilot.

## Plate logs are personal data under the 2026 law, with a wide police carve-out

Bangladesh moved from having no data protection law to a consent-based regime within a year, but the final text and its commencement remain partly unclear.

| Instrument | Date | Status (31 Oct 2026) | What it means for ALPR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Personal Data Protection Ordinance 2025 (No. 61) | Gazetted 6 Nov 2025 | Replaced by the 2026 Act, per secondary sources | Consent, purpose and retention notices |
| PDP (Amendment) Ordinance 2026 (No. 23) | 5 Feb 2026 | Folded into the Act | Critical Information Infrastructure (CII) bodies keep a real-time in-country copy of cloud data |
| Personal Data Protection Act 2026 (Act 63) | Passed April 2026 | Enacted; commencement not confirmed | Location data and identifiers are personal data; Section 24 state exemptions |
| National Data Governance Ordinance 2025 | 6 Nov 2025 | Reportedly replaced by a 2026 Act (single source) | National Data Management Authority; government data exchange |
| Cyber Security Ordinance 2025 (No. 25) | In force 21 May 2025 | In force; amended 2026 | CII designation; BTRC data requests |
| Road Transport Act 2018 | In force 2019 | In force | Plate display duty; fines up to Tk 50,000 |

The ordinances were gazetted on 6 November 2025. They require explicit consent before data is collected, stored, transferred or used, and they give people rights to access, correct and delete their data and to restrict automated decisions ([TBS/BSS, 9 Nov 2025](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/govt-issues-gazettes-2-landmark-ordinances-data-protection-governance-1281356)). Consent must be voluntary, specific, informed and withdrawable, and people must be told the purpose, retention period and recipients. The ordinance set an 18-month compliance window, pointing to enforcement around May 2027 ([BD-SCL, 23 Apr 2026](https://bd-scl.com/insights/personal-data-protection-ordinance-2025-compliance.html)). According to a vendor summary, Parliament's Personal Data Protection Act 2026 (Act 63 of 2026) includes **location data and online identifiers** in its definition of personal data. It also bars keeping data longer than necessary, requires processing records to be kept for at least five years, and requires breaches likely to cause significant damage to be reported to the Authority ([Securiti, 29 Jun 2026](https://securiti.ai/bangladesh-personal-data-protection-act-overview/)). A plate number tied to a time and place is therefore personal data. Penalties are unsettled in the sources: turnover-based fines of 1-5 % per a law firm and an opinion column, against fixed fines of up to Tk 25-50 lakh per Securiti. The Act's own text was not read, so commencement, whether the 18-month window survives, and the applicable fines must be checked against the gazette before any compliance claim is made.

The state carve-out is wide. Section 24 deems consent waived where processing is necessary for national security, defence, public order or an undefined public interest, and Section 49 makes the Authority follow government directions ([BD Law Digest, 18 Aug 2026](https://bdlawdigest.org/personal-data-protection-act-2026-bangladesh.html)). A barrister's op-ed adds that these exceptions come without necessity or proportionality safeguards ([TBS, 20 Apr 2026](https://www.tbsnews.net/thoughts/personal-data-protection-ordinance-law-protects-you-everyone-except-state-1416106)), and Transparency International Bangladesh flagged the same breadth ([The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/tech-startup/news/bangladeshs-personal-data-protection-ordinance-2025-key-takeaways-4015401)). DMP and BRTA will almost certainly process plate data under these exemptions. Private parking operators, malls, hospitals, factories and housing societies get no such shelter. They need signage or another form of notice, a lawful basis, a stated retention period and a way to handle data-subject requests. Localisation adds a design constraint. Data classed "confidential" or "restricted" must stay in Bangladesh ([BD-SCL](https://bd-scl.com/insights/personal-data-protection-ordinance-2025-compliance.html)), and bodies designated CII under the Cyber Security Ordinance must keep at least one synchronised real-time copy of cloud data in the country ([Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/38601-president-implemented-personal-data-protection-amendment-ordinance-2026-ordinance-no-23-of-2026)). A toll or traffic authority is a plausible CII designee. An edge unit that stores locally and reports to an in-country server avoids the question entirely, whatever link carries the data. The Cyber Security Ordinance 2025 has been in force since 21 May 2025 ([Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/38587-cyber-security-ordinance-2025-ordinance-no-25-of-2025-enters-into-force)), and a 2026 amendment lets BTRC and other agencies request user data from platforms ([Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/parliament/414016/cyber-security-amendment-bill-2026-passes-in)). Under the Road Transport Act 2018, driving without registration and a number plate carries a fine of up to Tk 50,000, six months in jail, or both ([TBS](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/penalties-violating-rules-under-new-road-transport-act)). **No published rule sets retention, access logging or signage for DMP's camera data** ([1001 Smart Cities](https://1001smartcities.org/projects/dhaka-e-traffic-prosecution/); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2648890/world)), and the Evidence Act's treatment of ANPR images was not researched.

International practice offers ready numbers to fill that gap. The UK ICO fixes no single period but requires minimum-necessary, purpose-bound retention justified by an impact assessment ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/cctv-and-video-surveillance/guidance-on-video-surveillance-including-cctv/additional-considerations-for-technologies-other-than-cctv/automatic-number-plate-recognition-anpr)). The EDPB says storage beyond 72 hours needs more justification ([EDPB Guidelines 3/2019](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sites/default/files/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_201903_video_devices_en.pdf)). Crawley's car-park ANPR images are kept on a 28-31 day cycle ([Crawley Borough Council](https://crawley.gov.uk/council-information/access-information/privacy-notices/other-privacy-notices/parking-anpr-and-cctv-privacy-notice)), and the UK's national police ANPR data is kept for 12 months ([North Yorkshire Police](https://northyorkshire.police.uk/foi-ai/north-yorkshire-police/foi-disclosure-2021-22/march-2025/foi-1089-202425-anpr/)). US states are tightening. Connecticut's 2026 SB 397 caps retention at 21 days, Washington's SB 6002 lists permitted uses, New Mexico's SB 40 restricts sharing, and Florida and Texas took executive action to remove or ban ALPRs on state roads ([MultiState, 23 Sep 2026](https://www.multistate.us/insider/2026/9/23/automatic-license-plate-readers-face-growing-state-restrictions-in-2026)). A defensible default for the project borrows from these. Reads with no hit are deleted on the device within 72 hours (enforcement) or 30 days (parking). Hits are kept until the case closes, and any longer archive exists only by statute. Use is limited to a written purpose list, and every access is logged. Counting should use keyed hashes (HMAC with rotating keys), because the Bangladeshi plate space of roughly two billion combinations is small enough that an unkeyed hash can be reversed by brute force. A human must confirm every hit before a notice goes out, which also respects the law's right to restrict automated decisions.

## A USD 4 billion market with no Bangla-native product

Market-research firms broadly agree on size and growth, though not on regional leadership. Precedence Research puts the global ANPR system market at **USD 4.14 billion in 2025 and USD 4.53 billion in 2026, rising to USD 9.27 billion by 2034 at a 9.36 % CAGR**. In its figures, traffic management is the largest application, government the largest end user, software the fastest-growing component (10.63 % CAGR) and Asia-Pacific the fastest-growing region (10.83 %) ([Precedence Research](https://www.precedenceresearch.com/automatic-number-plate-recognition-system-market)). MarketsandMarkets (9.2 %) and Grand View (9.3 % to USD 6.37 billion by 2030) give similar growth rates ([MarketsandMarkets](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/PressReleases/automatic-number-plate-recognition-system.asp); [GII listing of Grand View](https://www.giikorea.co.kr/report/grvi1587838-automatic-number-plate-recognition-system-market.html)). Which region is largest today is disputed: North America for Precedence, Europe for PS Market Research ([PS Market Research](https://www.psmarketresearch.com/press-release/automatic-number-plate-recognition-system-market)). No free source gives a Bangladesh or South Asia figure. These are publisher estimates, not audited data, but software-led growth and government demand both favour a local software team.

Bangladesh's supply side is fragmented, and nobody documents Bangla-plate accuracy. Tiger IT ran BRTA's retro-reflective plate, RFID and smart-registration project from 2016 ([Tiger IT](https://www.tigerit.com/)). Bangladesh Machine Tools Factory makes the plates, and BRTA paid it Tk 1,805-3,652 per plate set in an older report ([The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/frontpage/news/vehicle-tracking-system-fees-taken-service-not-ensured-1855579)). DMP's camera system is described as homegrown, but sources disagree on who built it, and no tender was found ([1001 Smart Cities](https://1001smartcities.org/projects/dhaka-e-traffic-prosecution/)). Padma tolling is run by Korea Expressway Corporation with a Chinese partner under a Tk 6.93 billion five-year contract ([Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/8ckvm0cvrk)). The Chinese-led Dhaka Elevated Expressway still collects tolls mostly by hand ([Financial Express](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/home/unified-e-toll-collection-system-unlikely-before-eid)). Hikvision ANPR cameras are sold at Ryans ([Ryans](https://www.ryans.com/category/security-camera-panel-ip-camera?page=8)), but no source confirms that they read Bangla. Plate Recognizer says its models are trained in over 90 countries, yet its pricing page mentions neither Bangladesh nor Bengali ([Plate Recognizer](https://platerecognizer.com/pricing/)).

Pricing benchmarks show where a student venture can and cannot compete. Plate Recognizer charges USD 50 a month for 50,000 cloud or on-premises lookups after a free tier of 2,500, USD 35 per camera a month for streams and USD 20 per camera for its ParkPow parking dashboard ([Plate Recognizer](https://platerecognizer.com/pricing/)). Nobody beats that on price per read. The openings are Bangla accuracy, low-cost edge hardware, in-country storage and privacy compliance. On the government side, DMP's pilot works out to about USD 7,100 per intersection (USD 50,000 across seven, servers and software included) ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2648890/world)). The hardware for one Pi-based reader costs roughly Tk 56,000-58,500 at local and list prices before enclosure, IR, router and power: the Pi 5 at Tk 35,650, the AI HAT+ at USD 110 (about Tk 13,400 at an assumed 122 Tk per dollar), a camera at Tk 4,273-6,450 and a power supply at Tk 2,950. That is the scale of DMP's request for cheaper plug-and-play units. Four business models follow. The first sells edge kits to government (DMP, city corporations, Highway Police) with an annual maintenance contract, the part past ITS projects lacked. The second is parking software below ParkPow's USD 20 per camera, with bKash and Nagad payment. The third is a per-lookup Bangla plate API for ride-hailing, insurers and fleet operators. The fourth is a compliance module for retention, hashing and audit logs. The risks are procurement through e-GP favouring established firms, customer unease over Section 24 data demands, localisation duties, and plate quality that no model can fix.

## Track fusion and calibrated abstention are the open Bangla niches

The team's file-backed results already sit at the brief's target, and its negative results point to the next contribution. On the 651-plate test set (run 26 October 2026, checked 27 October), **the four-reader vote reached 90.94 % plate-exact and char-YOLO with an SVTRv2-T fallback 90.17 %**, against 86.79 % for char-YOLO alone, 81.26 % for SVTRv2-T and 73.43 % for the CRNN. An oracle that always picked the right reader would reach 93.7 % (team notes). The heavy image-side fixes failed. SAHI slicing cut detector AP50 from 97.33 to 91.64 (team notes), and zero-shot HVI-CIDNet enhancement did not help night plates (team notes). A learned LPLCv2 legibility gate lost to plain CRNN confidence: 81.00 % against 84.45 % accuracy at 80 % coverage (team notes). In an earlier run on dataset v1.0, plate super-resolution beat bicubic upscaling by only 0.3 points (team notes). The pattern is consistent: **on Bangla plates, cheap signals from the readers beat expensive pixel restoration.** Published Bangla work has not moved past single-image pipelines. The newest, a YOLO plus vision-encoder-decoder paper at IEEE ICAD 2026, reports a CER of 0.1323 for ViT + BanglaBERT ([arXiv 2603.10267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10267)). That is a different metric on different data, so it is not a head-to-head comparison. None of the Bangla papers found uses multi-frame fusion, vision-language fallback, grammar decoding or calibrated review routing.

| Priority | Direction | Evidence that it works elsewhere | Why it is new for Bangla | Team's starting point |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Multi-frame track benchmark with track-level voting and logit fusion | ICPR 2026 low-resolution LPR competition; UFPR-SR-Plates +13.6 points; MF-LPR² 86.44 vs 14.04 % | No Bangla plate video dataset exists | Detector, five readers, and a video loop at 57-88 FPS |

| 3 | Grammar-constrained decoding | Layout masks used by the 2nd and 5th places at ICPR 2026 | Closed lists of districts, class letters, series 11-99 and numbers 0001-9999 | No reading with an invalid layout was ever correct |
| 4 | Confidence-gated small vision-language model fallback, then LoRA fine-tuning | VehiclePaliGemma 87.6 % vs GPT-4o 86 %; Bolivian Gemma3-4B fallback; PaddleOCR-VL-1.5 adds Bengali | No Bangla plate VLM benchmark | Reader disagreement is a free gate |
| 5 | Single-line motorcycle front-plate set | — | A new plate format arrives on 15 Nov 2026 | Motorcycle-heavy roads to film |
| 6 | Reader test-time adaptation for night | Test-time adaptation with an SVTR reader for industrial codes (2025) | No plate test-time adaptation paper found | Night slot OCR 27.7 %; enhancement failed |
| 7 | Rendered synthetic plates for rare districts | Synthetic and real data fusion (IET ITS 2025); diffusion +3 % | Rare district words starve training | Font rendering is CPU-cheap |
| 8 | Plate vs vehicle-attribute consistency for fake plates | UFPR-VeSV (2026); UK N8PRP report (2021) | Class letters encode vehicle type | Needs BRTA data for real validation |

**Multi-frame fusion is the strongest documented direction of 2025-2026.** In the ICPR 2026 low-resolution plate-recognition competition, 99 teams submitted valid entries. The winner scored 82.13 %, and the organisers found that the consistent factor among top entries was how well they exploited the five frames of each track. Two of the top three used no super-resolution at all. Their methods were cheap to copy: summing per-frame logits before decoding (1st), quality-weighted frame fusion (2nd) and character-level majority voting over 20 predictions (3rd) ([arXiv 2604.22506](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22506v2)). That independently matches the team's finding that restoration does not pay. On UFPR-SR-Plates, fusing the OCR outputs of five frames added 13.6 points over a single super-resolved image ([arXiv 2505.06393](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06393)). MF-LPR² lifted recognition on dash-cam sequences from 14.04 % to 86.44 % ([arXiv 2508.14797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14797)). There is a testable open question. Across models, Laroca's model-fusion study found sequence-level voting best ([arXiv 2309.04331](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.04331)). Across frames, UFPR-SR-Plates found character-position voting best. The team's own earlier plate-SR run (dataset v1.0) found sequence-level majority voting (MV-HC, 32.10 %) beating character-position voting (team notes). Which rule holds for two-line Bangla plates is unknown. The big published gains come from low-resolution data, and the gain at the team's roughly 91 % single-frame level is unknown. That makes a released **Bangla multi-frame track benchmark** valuable whether the gain turns out large or small, and its main cost is filming and labelling one string per track, not GPU time.

**Calibrated abstention is the cheapest novel contribution, and the one that matters most in the field.** The team's legibility study already found that when char-YOLO and the CRNN agree, on 72.8 % of plates, 97.3 % of readings are correct (team notes). Split conformal prediction turns that kind of signal into a guarantee: with a held-out calibration split, it states the review rate needed to keep auto-accepted plates above a target accuracy, say 99 %. It has been applied to document key-information extraction ([IJDAR 2026](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10032-026-00572-y)) and to table OCR ([arXiv 2507.02009](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02009v1)), but no plate-specific conformal paper was found. The ICPR 2026 organisers reported that confidence calibration varied widely between teams of similar accuracy, with confidence gaps from 2.38 % to 14.86 % ([arXiv 2604.22506](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22506v2)). An ICIP 2026 challenge entry scored +2 for correct reads, −1 for wrong and 0 for abstaining ([arXiv 2607.08896](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.08896)), a ready-made protocol for enforcement, where a wrong notice costs more than no notice.

Grammar-constrained decoding pairs naturally with abstention. In the team's model-fusion and SVTRv2 studies, no reader was ever right when it produced an invalid BRTA layout (team notes; team notes), so a lexicon or constrained beam search over the closed plate grammar costs almost nothing and should lift the weaker sequence readers most. A vision-language fallback then handles what remains. VehiclePaliGemma, fine-tuned on only 600 synthetic Malaysian plates, reached 87.6 % on 258 hard real plates, ahead of GPT-4o's 86 %, at 0.135 s per plate on an A100 ([Scientific Reports, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12274586/)). A 2026 Bolivian system calls Gemma3-4B only on low-confidence plates ([arXiv 2604.09927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09927)), and PaddleOCR-VL-1.5 added Bengali in January 2026 ([arXiv 2601.21957](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.21957)). Generative readers and restorers can invent plausible but wrong digits, as Diffusion-SR3 did at extreme angles ([arXiv 2604.23814](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.23814)), so their output must pass the grammar check and the abstention layer. The front-plate mandate opens a time-limited data opportunity. From 15 November 2026 Dhaka's roads will carry a new, probably single-line motorcycle plate (285 × 50 mm) that no model has seen, and a team that films it first owns that dataset. Lower priorities include reader test-time adaptation for night plates ([arXiv 2502.09026](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09026v1)), rendered synthetic plates for rare districts ([arXiv 2601.07671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07671)), plate-vehicle consistency for fake plates ([arXiv 2604.05271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05271)) and federated training, which a BRAC thesis has already explored for Bangladesh ([BRAC DSpace](https://dspace.bracu.ac.bd/items/acac5760-93de-4377-8c18-437f1550d66f)). A field deployment on solar power with satellite backhaul could be a further system-level first, since the search found no documented solar or satellite ALPR deployment in South Asia, and Bangladesh's own highway cameras do not disclose their backhaul ([TBS/BSS](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/1539861)). That needs a more careful literature check before anyone claims it.

## Conclusion

The binding constraint on plate recognition in Bangladesh is identity infrastructure, not detector accuracy. RFID failed even though the technology works elsewhere. DMP's visible errors come from untransferred ownership records and plateless vehicles, and three generations of donor-funded signal projects failed or never ran. A student system will therefore be judged on cost per approach, maintainability and above all **what it does when it is unsure**, since wrong notices, scam calls impersonating DMP and wrongful US detentions show that a confident wrong read does more harm than a missing one. The unmeasured facts that matter most are DMP's real read accuracy, the team's best reader on actual Pi 5 hardware, the text and commencement of the 2026 Act, and local hardware prices.

The insight that ties the research to the country's needs is that **calibrated abstention is at once the most publishable contribution, the compliance mechanism and the trust mechanism**. One layer (multi-frame voting, grammar validity and a conformal review threshold) answers three demands: a novel Bangla paper, the data law's right to restrict automated decisions, and DMP's need to file cases at scale without sending them to the wrong people. The timing is unusually good. Front plates arrive on 15 November 2026, DMP and Highway Police are scaling to hundreds of camera points, and private operators face data-law compliance from around 2027. A flagship paper titled roughly "A Bangla multi-frame plate benchmark with calibrated abstention, measured on a solar-capable Pi 5 + Hailo unit" would capture all three at once.
