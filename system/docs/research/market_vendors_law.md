Scope note: all figures below come from free web summaries, news reports and vendor pages read on 31 Oct 2026. Market-research numbers are publisher estimates, not audited data, and they use different scopes and base years. Where a fact rests on one secondary source, that is said next to it.

## Q1. Global and Asia-Pacific ALPR/ANPR market size, growth and segment split

### Takeaway
Most publishers put the global ANPR system market at roughly USD 3.1-4.5 billion around 2024-2026, growing about 9-10 % a year, reaching about USD 6-9 billion by 2030-2034. Asia-Pacific is named the fastest-growing region by several firms, but publishers disagree on which region is largest today. Traffic management is the largest application; free summaries do not give a clean parking / tolling / law-enforcement split.

## Q2. Who supplies ALPR, CCTV, ITS, tolling, RFID and smart parking in Bangladesh, and do they read Bangla plates?

### Takeaway
Bangladesh's plate and RFID programme was built by local firm Tiger IT (with plates made via BMTF), but RFID has not delivered its promised uses. The first city-scale ANPR enforcement is the Dhaka Metropolitan Police (DMP) "AI-Based Road Transport Act 2018 Violation Detection System", live since 7 May 2026 and described as homegrown. Toll operations on the Padma Bridge corridor are run by Korean/Chinese contractors; the Dhaka Elevated Expressway is still mostly manual. No international commercial ALPR vendor was found to document Bangla-script plate support.

## Q3. Bangladesh law: data protection, data governance, cyber security, road transport, evidence (status as of 31 Oct 2026)

### Takeaway
Bangladesh now has a consent-based personal data law: the Personal Data Protection Ordinance 2025 (No. 61 of 2025, gazetted 6 Nov 2025), amended by Ordinance No. 23 of 2026 (5 Feb 2026), and reportedly replaced by the Personal Data Protection Act 2026 (Act 63 of 2026, passed April 2026). It has wide state exemptions (Section 24), localisation for "confidential/restricted" data and CII, and a regulator under government direction. The Cyber Security Ordinance 2025 is in force (and was amended by Parliament in 2026). The Road Transport Act 2018 is the basis of camera-based fines.

Personal data protection
- Both ordinances (PDPO 2025 and National Data Governance Ordinance 2025) gazetted by the Law Ministry on 6 Nov 2025, announced 9 Nov; approved by the Council of Advisers on 9 Oct. Explicit consent needed before collection, storage, transfer or use; citizens are "owners" of their data; rights to access, correct, delete and restrict automated decisions; enhanced protection for financial, health, genetic and biometric data; profiling/behavioural tracking of minors banned; a National Data Management Authority (NDMA) set up under the Data Governance Ordinance; a National Responsible Data Exchange (NRDEX) for government and private data sharing, a Unified Digital Identity and a National Source Code Repository — [TBS / BSS, 9 Nov 2025](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/govt-issues-gazettes-2-landmark-ordinances-data-protection-governance-1281356)
- PDPO is Ordinance No. 61 of 2025 (English text copy) — [DPO India PDF](https://www.dpo-india.com/Resources/Privacy_Regulations_in_Asia_Pacific_Countries/Bangladesh-Personal-Data-Protection-Ordinance,2025(Ordinance.No.61-2025).pdf)
- Law-firm briefing (23 Apr 2026): 18-month compliance window from gazette, so enforcement about May 2027; Section 6 consent must be voluntary, specific, informed, unambiguous and withdrawable, and data subjects must be told purpose, retention period, transfers/recipients and how to withdraw; "confidential" and "restricted" data must be stored in Bangladesh; other transfers only to "adequate" jurisdictions (standard not yet notified); fiduciary liable for its processors; fines 1-2 % of annual turnover, 2-5 % for "significant data fiduciaries"; no PDPO cases reported by April 2026 — [BD-SCL](https://bd-scl.com/insights/personal-data-protection-ordinance-2025-compliance.html)
- Amendment Ordinance No. 23 of 2026 promulgated 5 Feb 2026; for entities designated Critical Information Infrastructure (CII) under the Cyber Security Ordinance 2025, at least one synchronised real-time copy of cloud data must be kept inside Bangladesh — [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/38601-president-implemented-personal-data-protection-amendment-ordinance-2026-ordinance-no-23-of-2026)
- Opinion by barrister Shafqat Aziz (TBS, 20 Apr 2026): the Feb 2026 amendment removed jail terms for company officers and set fines of up to 5 % of annual turnover; localisation was eased and limited to critical sectors such as banking and healthcare; Section 24 allows exceptions for national security, public order and crime prevention without definitions or necessity/proportionality safeguards; the NDMA reports to the Prime Minister's Office — [TBS](https://www.tbsnews.net/thoughts/personal-data-protection-ordinance-law-protects-you-everyone-except-state-1416106)
- Transparency International Bangladesh flagged broad exemptions in subsection 15(4) and Section 24 (as reported in coverage) — [Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/bangladeshs-personal-data-protection-ordinance-2025-key-takeaways/); [The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/tech-startup/news/bangladeshs-personal-data-protection-ordinance-2025-key-takeaways-4015401)
- Securiti (vendor summary, 29 Jun 2026): Parliament passed the Personal Data Protection Act 2026 (Law 63 of 2026) in April 2026, repealing the PDPO; personal data explicitly includes location data and online identifiers; data may not be kept longer than necessary (except public interest, research, statistics); processing records kept at least 5 years; breaches likely to cause "significant damage" reported to the Authority (timeframe left to regulations); extraterritorial; large-scale transfers of sensitive PII must be notified and the Authority can suspend transfers; fines up to Tk 25 lakh (negligence; failing data-subject rights) and up to Tk 50 lakh for significant data fiduciaries; significant controllers appoint a Chief Data Officer — [Securiti](https://securiti.ai/bangladesh-personal-data-protection-act-overview/)
- Act 2026 classifies data into four classes: public open, internal, confidential, restricted; one source dates passage to 15 Apr 2026 (search-result summaries of academic papers; not read in full) — [Semantic Scholar PDF](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/f4fe/9049104e124d7f5a8a7f56d4fae089a12ee8.pdf); [Legal Research Analysis](https://legalresearchanalysis.com/LRA/article/view/195)
- Student analysis (18 Aug 2026): under the Act, Section 24 deems consent waived where processing is "necessary" for national security, defence, public order and an undefined "public interest"; Section 49 makes the Authority follow government directions — [BD Law Digest](https://bdlawdigest.org/personal-data-protection-act-2026-bangladesh.html)
- SSRN working paper says Parliament enacted both the Personal Data Protection Act 2026 and a National Data Management Act 2026, replacing the ordinances (single source) — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7436740)
- Constitutional privacy clause (Article 43) is argued to be too narrow for digital data — [Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/why-bangladeshs-new-data-protection-law-may-fail-to-protect-your-data/)
- Conflict on penalties: turnover-based fines (1-5 %) per BD-SCL and TBS vs fixed fines up to Tk 25-50 lakh per Securiti; the Act text was not read, so which applies now is unconfirmed — sources above.

### Inferences
- A plate number linked to time and place is "personal data" under the Act's definition (identifiers plus location data), so a private ALPR operator (parking, malls, gated housing) would need notice and consent or another lawful basis, a stated retention period, and rights handling. Government policing will likely rely on the Section 24 exemptions.
- Cloud/Starlink: if plate logs are classed "confidential" or "restricted", or the client is a CII entity (e.g. a toll or traffic authority), data must stay in Bangladesh (in-country storage or at least a real-time in-country copy). Edge processing with local storage is the low-risk design.
- The Act's turnover-based or fixed fines would hit a startup's B2B customers, so privacy-by-design (short retention, hashing of non-hit plates, access logs) is a sales feature.
- Because the 18-month window was tied to the 2025 Ordinance, the compliance deadline under the 2026 Act (if any) must be checked in the Act text.

### Gaps
- The text of the Personal Data Protection Act 2026 on bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd was not read; commencement date, whether the 18-month window survives, breach-notification hours, the fines that apply and the exact data-class rules for location/vehicle data are not confirmed.
- Whether the National Data Governance Ordinance 2025 became a "National Data Management Act 2026" rests on one SSRN paper.
- Whether the Cyber Security Ordinance 2025 itself was converted into an Act (the 2026 amendment bill implies it is still in force) was not confirmed.
- The Evidence Act (and any 2022 amendment on digital evidence) was not researched in this pass; no source collected on admissibility rules for ANPR images.
- Exact section numbers of the Road Transport Act 2018 for plate offences and camera evidence were not confirmed.
- No Bangladesh rule on CCTV retention, signage or ANPR specifically was found.

## Q4. International good practice on ALPR retention and privacy that Bangladesh could copy

### Takeaway
The UK ICO and EDPB do not fix one retention period; they require purpose-bound, minimum-necessary retention justified by a DPIA. Real-world benchmarks: about 28-31 days for car-park ANPR images (UK council), 12 months for UK national police ANPR data, 72 hours as the EU point where storage needs extra justification, and 21 days for non-investigative data in several US states.

### Cited Findings
- UK ICO: ANPR systems need retention periods consistent with the purpose and data kept only for the minimum period necessary; comply with the Surveillance Camera Code of Practice where required — [ICO ANPR guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/cctv-and-video-surveillance/guidance-on-video-surveillance-including-cctv/additional-considerations-for-technologies-other-than-cctv/automatic-number-plate-recognition-anpr)

- UK councils: Crawley car-park ANPR images kept on a 28-31 day cycle — [Crawley Borough Council](https://crawley.gov.uk/council-information/access-information/privacy-notices/other-privacy-notices/parking-anpr-and-cctv-privacy-notice); Hampshire ANPR data normally deleted after 12 months — [Hampshire County Council](https://www.hants.gov.uk/thingstodo/calshot/visit/ANPR-code-of-practise); Coventry keeps non-contravention data 24 months for a DVLA audit requirement — [Coventry DPIA](https://coventry.gov.uk/downloads/file/35352/ico-dpia-parking-and-bus-lane-enforcement)

### Inferences
- A copyable Bangladesh policy: non-hit plate reads deleted within 72 hours to 30 days (EDPB / UK car-park practice); hits tied to a fine or case kept until the case closes; any longer police archive (UK 12 months) only by law; purpose list as in Washington SB 6002; no sharing outside stated purposes as in New Mexico SB 40; access audit logs and a DPIA as in ICO guidance.

## Q5. Business models and pricing for a student ALPR startup in Bangladesh

### Takeaway
Global cloud/SDK ALPR is cheap per read (Plate Recognizer from free to about USD 0.0005-0.001 per lookup, or USD 20-45 per camera per month), so a Bangladeshi startup cannot win on price alone; it can win on Bangla-plate accuracy, low-cost edge hardware, local data storage and privacy compliance. Clear local buyers are DMP (500-camera target, says cameras too costly), BRTA (front plates, duplicate plates), toll operators and parking sites.

### Cited Findings
- Plate Recognizer pricing (USD): Snapshot API (cloud or on-prem SDK, same price) free 2,500 lookups/month; USD 50/month for 50,000; USD 150 for 250,000; USD 250 for 500,000; make-model-colour adds 50 %. Stream USD 35 per camera/month (USD 45 with MMC), 3 cameras free for 1 month. ParkPow parking dashboard USD 20 per camera/month. Gate opener USD 10 per gate/month. Annual plans save two months — [Plate Recognizer pricing](https://platerecognizer.com/pricing/)
- DMP pilot: about USD 50,000 for seven intersections, half on software — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2648890/world); DMP target 500 cameras in six months, says cameras are too costly and wants cheaper "plug-and-play" units; about 25 major intersections need 24-hour management — [1001 Smart Cities](https://1001smartcities.org/projects/dhaka-e-traffic-prosecution/)
- DMP daily report, 26 Aug 2026: 2,431 prosecutions, Tk 6,517,425 assessed, Tk 4,845,025 realised across eight divisions (camera and officer cases not separated) — [1001 Smart Cities](https://1001smartcities.org/projects/dhaka-e-traffic-prosecution/)
- Motorcycle front-plate mandate: about 5.1 million motorcycles; if front plates cost the same as rear (Tk 2,260), total cost would exceed Tk 11.52 bn — [Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/f5iiqyoxwt)
- Toll market size signals: Padma Bridge toll revenue Tk 30 bn — [Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/9l6ijuky9i); KEC five-year toll/maintenance contract Tk 6.93 bn — [Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/8ckvm0cvrk); Dhaka Elevated Expressway still mostly manual — [The Financial Express](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/home/unified-e-toll-collection-system-unlikely-before-eid)
- Smart parking in Bangladesh is app-led so far: DNCC 202 street spots (Gulshan) — [The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/tech-startup/news/dncc-launches-smart-parking-app-3464746); B-Trac "Yes Parking" at the Military Museum — [Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/corporate/a86477njbz); MicrotechBD sells ANPR car-park integration — [MicrotechBD](https://www.microtechbd.net/intelligent-car-parking-solution.php)
- Software is the fastest-growing ANPR segment globally (10.63 % CAGR) and government the largest end-user — [Precedence Research](https://www.precedenceresearch.com/automatic-number-plate-recognition-system-market)

### Inferences
- Model A, B2G edge kit: low-cost Bangla ANPR box retrofitted to DMP/city-corporation signal cameras, sold per intersection with an annual maintenance contract; benchmark is the DMP pilot cost of about USD 7,000 per intersection (USD 50,000 / 7).
- Model B, B2B parking SaaS: per-camera monthly fee priced below ParkPow's USD 20, with Bangla plates, bKash/Nagad payment and local storage; targets malls, hospitals, office towers, gated housing, university campuses.
- Model C, API per lookup: a Bangla plate-reading API for fintech/insurance/ride-hailing/fleet apps, priced near Plate Recognizer's tier (USD 50 per 50,000 reads) but with better Bangla accuracy.
- Model D, compliance add-on: retention, hashing and audit-log module that helps private operators meet the Personal Data Protection Act 2026.
- Main risks: Section 24 state-access exemptions (customers may worry about forced data sharing), localisation rules for cloud/Starlink, procurement through e-GP favouring established firms, and plate quality (duplicate/fake plates, plateless vehicles).

- No public price found for Hikvision/Dahua ANPR cameras in Bangladesh (Ryans listing seen but price not captured).

- No revenue or user figures found for DNCC Smart Parking or Yes Parking.
